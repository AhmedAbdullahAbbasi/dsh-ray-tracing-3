**Result: PASS for the supplied 4.9-keV, NH=1e22 cm^-2, time-integrated halo.**
This establishes agreement of the absolute halo normalization and nine-bin

## Material and estimator matching

The material pair uses the supplied executable's exported opacities and
inspected analytic deflection sampler. It is a compatibility implementation,
not an independently generated full Mie differential-scattering calculation.
The earlier adapter report documents the binary-specific energy lookup.

The total dust opacity is split with its albedo into scattering and grain
absorption. Depleted gas absorption is added; the full TBabs opacity is not added.
At 4.9 keV the runtime-matched coefficients are approximately
sigma_sca=1.97720e-24 and sigma_abs=2.48326e-24 cm^2/H.

Every virtual observer contribution comes from the unchanged production launch,
transport and peel-off functions. Before image cropping, a uniform circular
aperture offset is applied, using each scattering location's distance to the
observer. The midpoint blur radius is approximately 4.13 arcsec. This avoids
losing photons that enter the image through the aperture response.

The fixed source/camera conversion is

    N0 = N_generated * sum(camera_solid_angle) / source_cone_solid_angle

with N0=328288.749600. Multiplying DSH halo fluence for
a unit source by N0 predicts RefleX camera counts. The direct beam supplies an
independent transmission check, not a fitted normalization.

The source is placed on the corner of the four central pixels, as established
by the supplied parameter-generation code and direct image. The direct counts
are checked to remain confined to those pixels. The RefleX Y axis is reflected
for the image display. Statistics use the same fixed pixel-center annular masks
on both images; the outermost mask includes the square-field corners.

## Uncertainty and fixed acceptance gates

Repeated scores from each photon are grouped before squaring. The saved
nine-by-nine radial cross moments include cross-bin covariance, and zero-score
histories remain in the sample size. Independent seed images are averaged,
with moments scaled consistently. Reference camera counts use the fixed-history
multinomial covariance, practically identical to independent Poisson variance
at these detection probabilities.

The joint radial test uses the sum of RefleX count covariance and DSH history
covariance. It has nine degrees of freedom and zero fitted parameters.
The thresholds were fixed in `comparison_config.json` before the main runs:

- Aperture: within 3 combined SE plus a 0.3% geometry/response allowance.
- Every annulus: within 4 combined SE plus that allowance.
- Joint radial and seed-consistency p-values >= 0.001.
- DSH aperture relative SE <= 0.6%; at least three independent seeds;
  at least 20 RefleX counts in every annulus.
- No active, interaction-capped, invalid-energy or invalid-state terminals.
- First plus higher-order image closure; direct transmission within 3 SE.

**All gates pass. The aperture and every annulus also pass without using the
0.3% allowance.** Seed consistency p=0.3021.

## Multiple scattering and scope

The saved DSH all-order image includes second and higher scattering. Their
fluence adds **0.962% relative to first order**.
There are no transport caps among the six million main histories.

The supplied RefleX dust image merges all scattering orders. This benchmark
therefore does not separately validate order two or order three. Its roughly
1.3% reference counting precision is also comparable to the approximately 1%
higher-order addition. A higher-opacity, order-resolved RefleX comparison is
needed for a strong external multiple-scattering test.

Other outstanding scopes include external comparisons at 3.3/6.9 keV,
continuous-energy interpolation, stronger optical depths, time-resolved echoes,
heterogeneous clouds, and an independently generated WD01/Mie material option.
The generic transport contains no telescope response; the finite aperture here
models RefleX's camera estimator. The figures are visual diagnostics; no
full-resolution per-pixel goodness-of-fit claim is made.
